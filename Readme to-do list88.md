👋 Welcome

I've tried every to-do app that exists. Every one of them eventually asked me to sign up for something. A workspace. A team. A "Pro" plan that unlocks recurring tasks. A sync that quietly uploads my shopping list to a server in Virginia. And after enough of that, I stopped opening them. The list I actually used was the back of an envelope.

This is that envelope, rebuilt properly.

It's a to-do list that lives in one HTML file. You type a task, press Enter, and it appears. You check it off, it fades. You can filter, edit, delete, clear what you've done, and that's it. Everything is saved in your browser's local storage — no account, no sync, no cloud, no notification asking if you really want to complete that task.

But it's not just a list. There are three ways to look at it, and switching between them changes the entire mood of the screen:

· List — the default. White paper on a warm beige desk. Georgia serif title, thin divider lines, the quiet page you'd actually write on. Feels like something you'd find on a designer's moodboard.
· Sticky note — the whole app becomes a yellow Post-it, tilted a degree and a half, with a strip of tape across the top and a cursive font that looks like your own handwriting. It sits on a dark wooden desk at night. It's the note you stick to your monitor.
· Widget — a dark glass card, indigo accents, circular checkboxes, iPhone-Settings energy. It floats on a deep blue background and looks like something that would live on your phone's home screen.

Same tasks, same data, three completely different feelings. The one you choose is remembered.

✨ What you'll find

Three views, one button apart.
In the top-right corner, a small switch with three tiny icons — a list, a sticky note, a phone widget. Click one, and the whole app morphs into that style. The layout, fonts, colors, borders, checkboxes, even the shape of the buttons all change. Your choice is saved, so next time you open the file it looks the way you left it.

Add a task by pressing Enter.
Type into the field at the top, hit Enter (or click Add), and the task appears at the top of the list with a soft drop-in animation. That's the entire input flow. No category picker, no date, no priority, no labels. Just the task and the fact that you wrote it down.

Check it off and watch it fade.
Every task has a checkbox on the left. Click it, and the text turns grey and gets a strikethrough. The checkbox fills with a checkmark. The item stays where it is, in the list, exactly where you left it — not hidden, not moved to another tab. You can see what you've done and what's left, side by side.

Edit a task in place.
Double-click any task's text — or hover and click the pencil icon — and it becomes an editable field, right there in the line. Change the words, press Enter, done. Press Escape to cancel. If you clear the text entirely and hit Enter, the task is deleted.

Delete without ceremony.
Hover over any task and a small red trash can appears on the right. Click it, the task is gone. No confirmation, no undo, no "Are you sure?" The whole point of this thing is that it doesn't get in your way.

Filters for the three things you actually want.
Below the list: All, Active, Done. Click one, and the list shows only those. The active filter has a small underline. That's the entire filtering system, and it's enough.

A progress bar you barely notice.
A thin horizontal line under the list fills from left to right as you complete tasks. At zero completed, it's empty. All done, it's full. It sits quietly above the filters and doesn't shout. You'll look at it when you want to, and ignore it the rest of the time.

A live count of what's left.
Bottom left of the footer: "3 items left." Plural handled correctly. This is the number that matters — not how many you've completed, but how many are still waiting for you.

One button to clear what's done.
Bottom right: Clear completed. It removes every finished task in one click, and stays disabled (faded, unclickable) until there's at least one to clear. So you can't accidentally wipe anything that isn't already done.

Your work survives refreshes.
Every task is written to your browser's localStorage the moment it's added, edited, checked, or deleted. Close the tab, reopen the file tomorrow, and your list is exactly where you left it — tasks, order, done states, and even your chosen view mode.

Cross-tab sync, quietly.
Open the file in two browser tabs, and edits in one update the other. Add a task in tab A and it appears in tab B. Check one off in tab B and tab A reflects it. It's not a feature you'll use every day, but it's the kind of detail that makes the whole thing feel alive.

🧭 How it works

1. Open the file.
One HTML file, no build step, no install, no account. Double-click it and the list appears in whatever view you used last — or the paper-white List view if it's your first time.

2. Write down what you have to do.
Type it into the field at the top and press Enter. The task appears at the top of the list, with a soft animation. That's step one, and it's the only step you need.

3. Work the list.
Click a checkbox to mark something done. Double-click the text to edit it. Hover to reveal the trash can and delete it. Use the filters below the list to see just what you need — active tasks only, done tasks only, or everything at once.

4. Watch the bar fill.
The thin progress bar above the filters fills as you complete tasks. It's a small, quiet read on how the day is going, without any badges or congratulatory messages or confetti.

5. Switch views whenever you want.
Tap one of the three icons in the top-right corner and the whole thing morphs into that style. List, Sticky note, Widget. Your tasks travel with you. So does your choice — the app remembers which one you were using last.

6. Clean up when you're done.
Once you've checked off a few things, click Clear completed. Every finished task disappears in one go, and the list is ready for tomorrow.

That's the whole app. There's no settings page, no account, no theme editor, no pro tier. It's a list and three ways of looking at it.

🛠️ A few small helps

"Where is my data stored?"
Entirely in your browser's localStorage, on your own device. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is synced to a cloud, nothing leaves your machine. If you clear your browser data, or open the file on a different device, your tasks won't follow you. This is a list that lives where you do.

"Can I use it on my phone?"
Yes. The layout is fully responsive and shrinks gracefully — the three views all have their own mobile-specific sizing. On touch devices, the edit and delete buttons are always visible, since there's no hover on a touchscreen.

"What's the difference between the three views?"
They're the same list, dressed differently. List is the default — paper white, serif title, thin dividers. Sticky note turns it into a yellow Post-it with a handwritten font, tilted slightly on a dark wooden desk. Widget is a dark glass card with indigo accents and circular checkboxes, floating on a deep background. Same data, three moods.

"Which view should I use?"
Whichever one you'd actually open. List is best for focus work and long lists. Sticky note is good for a short, visible set of daily tasks you want to stare at. Widget is the one that looks nicest on a phone screen or on a desktop alongside other windows. Try each for a day and see which one you keep coming back to.

"Why did my tasks disappear?"
Two possibilities. Either you cleared your browser data — some browsers clear localStorage after long periods of inactivity, or when you use private browsing — or you clicked Clear completed and forgot that everything was already checked off. There's no undo and no history, so treat it like a paper list: keep a copy if it matters.

"What if I want two separate lists?"
You don't get them here, and that's intentional. One list, one view, one place to check. If you need a separate shopping list and a separate work list, open two copies of the file in different folders — each one gets its own localStorage because each one runs in its own browser origin (or in its own tab, if you're using file:// — behavior varies by browser).

"Can I reorder tasks?"
Not by dragging. New tasks go to the top, older tasks sit below. If you want to move something, edit the text to reflect its place, or delete it and re-add it. Drag-and-drop reordering would mean a whole new layer of interaction, and the point of this app is to stay out of your way.

"Does it work offline?"
Completely. Once the file is open, nothing needs the network. There are no external fonts, no CDN scripts, no icons loaded from a server — everything is inline. You can open it on a plane, in a basement, or on a desert island, and it will work exactly the same.

"The cross-tab sync isn't working."
It uses the browser's storage event, which only fires when two tabs are on the same origin. If you opened the file from your filesystem (file://), some browsers treat each tab as its own origin and won't sync. Serve it from a local web server, or from any URL, and it works perfectly.

"Can I change the font or the colors?"
Not through the interface. Open the file in a text editor and look for the three mode blocks near the bottom of the <style> section — mode-list, mode-sticky, mode-widget. Each one controls its own appearance. Fonts, colors, corner radii, the tilt of the sticky note — all editable, all local.

"Is there a keyboard shortcut to add a task?"
Not a specific one. The input field gets focus when you open the page, so you can start typing immediately. Press Enter to save, and the field clears and refocuses so you can add the next one without touching the mouse.
